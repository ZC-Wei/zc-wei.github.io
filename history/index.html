Our story

Faithfully present in Blackrock for more than a century.

From a small group gathering in the town hall to a church community rooted in worship and service, St Andrew’s has been part of Blackrock’s story since 1895.

1895

A history of St Andrew’s

A congregation takes root.

In July 1894, Presbyterians living in the Blackrock area met in the town hall to consider founding a congregation. Their request was approved by the General Assembly in June 1895, and Blackrock Presbyterian Church came into being.

The new congregation soon chose Albion House on Merrion Avenue as its home. Later that year, Mr Hewat proposed the name that has endured: St Andrew’s Presbyterian Church.

Milestones

Some moments from our first hundred years.

These highlights are drawn from G. Henderson’s account of St Andrew’s history, first published for the centenary.

1894–95

A new community begins

A small group worshipped in Blackrock’s town hall while making plans for a new Presbyterian congregation. James Snowdon, who would become the church’s first minister, was appointed and ordained in 1895.

1898–99

A church is built

The foundation stone was laid in October 1898. The first service in the new octagonal church was held on 5 February 1899; the communion table and baptismal font given at that time still grace the church.

1912

Music for worship

With the congregation’s support and a contribution from Andrew Carnegie, an organ was installed in the church alcove, enriching worship for generations to come.

1949

Renewal after the war

Following repairs to the church floor, St Andrew’s reopened and was rededicated in June 1949. A central aisle and a new home for the organ reshaped the interior.

1959

A hall for the community

The present church hall opened in December 1959. It gave fresh energy to the congregation and has since welcomed meetings, groups and community activity.

1995

One hundred years

For the centenary, the church interior was redecorated, the stonework outside restored and the Centenary Room created in the hall—a practical sign of confidence in the future.

Looking forward

Rooted in a long story. Open to what comes next.

St Andrew’s continues to share the love of God and the gospel of Jesus Christ through worship, fellowship and involvement in the Blackrock community.
